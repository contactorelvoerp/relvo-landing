# Risk Reversal, Scarcity and Urgency

## Part 1 — Guarantees (risk reversal)

Goal: shift risk from the customer to the business. The stronger the guarantee, the higher the perceived likelihood of success and the more sales — usually more than refunds cost.

### Guarantee types

| Type | Description | When to use | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unconditional | No-questions-asked refund within X days | Low variable costs, digital products, highly reliable product | "30 days to try it. Don't love it — get 100% back, no questions asked." |
| Conditional (outcome-based) | Refund/extra work if the customer completes the steps and doesn't get the result | Services, programs, coaching — where results depend on engagement | "Implement the 5 steps and don't get 10 leads in 30 days? We keep working with you for free until you do." |
| Anti-guarantee | Explicitly no refunds, with a reason | Inherently non-refundable services, access to confidential material, custom work | "No refunds, because from day one you get full access to …" |
| Implied | Pay for performance: commission, revenue share, pay only after results | When the outcome is measurable | "You pay only once the campaign brings in its first 50 inquiries." |

### Forms of compensation
Refund; work until it works; extra month free; refund + keep the bonuses; double refund (only with a very reliable product and user approval).

### Guarantee stacking
Combine them, e.g. an unconditional 14-day guarantee at the start + a conditional outcome guarantee at 90 days.

### Rules for writing guarantees
- Name it ("The 10-Lead Guarantee").
- State: timeframe, conditions (specific customer actions), what exactly the customer gets.
- Conditions must be achievable and meaningful — they should motivate the customer to act, not be a trap.
- Place it next to the price, next to the CTA, and in the FAQ.
- Always get user approval. If the business won't offer a refund, propose a "we keep working" guarantee or an anti-guarantee.
- Don't confuse a commercial guarantee with statutory consumer rights (e.g. in the EU: statutory warranty, 14-day right of withdrawal for distance sales) — the offer's guarantee is on top of the legal minimum. When in doubt, recommend the user have their terms reviewed by a lawyer.

### Bonuses as support for risk reversal
Bonuses that raise the chance of success (1:1 onboarding, ready-made templates, implementation checklists, progress check-ins) make the guarantee safe for the business — customers reach the outcome more often.

## Part 2 — Scarcity and urgency

**Scarcity** = limited quantity (spots, slots, units). **Urgency** = limited time (deadline).

### Overriding rule: only real constraints
Fake scarcity (a countdown that resets on refresh, a permanent "only 2 spots left") misleads customers, destroys trust, and is an unfair commercial practice in the EU and many other jurisdictions. If the business has no natural constraint, help **design** a real one, and then stick to it.

### Sources of scarcity
- Client limit per period based on team capacity ("we take on 8 companies per month")
- Seat limit per cohort/group
- Unit limit (limited edition, production batch)
- Bonus limit ("for the first 20 people")
- Price limit ("launch price for the first 50 customers")

### Sources of urgency
- Cohorts with a start date ("enrollment closes October 15")
- Season / occasion ("before the holiday season")
- Bonus deadline (core offer stays, bonus goes away)
- Announced price increase with a date
- Expiring individual offer (e.g. valid 72 h after a consultation)

### Reason why
Always give the reason for the constraint: service quality, individual attention, production capacity, group start. The reason makes the constraint believable.

### On-site implementation
- Mark dynamic values (spots left, dates) with variables such as `[SPOTS_LEFT]`, `[ENROLLMENT_CLOSE_DATE]` and tell the user where to update them (config, CMS, API).
- If you use a countdown timer — only with a real, fixed end date, identical for all visitors.
- Communicate the constraint in or just below the hero, next to the price, and at the final CTA.
