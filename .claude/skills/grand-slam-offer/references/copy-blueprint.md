# Website Copy Blueprint

All copy is written in the copy language chosen in Step 1a of SKILL.md.

## A. Landing page (single sales page)

The section order enforces "value first, price later".

1. **Hero**
   - Headline: dream outcome + timeframe (+ optionally "without …")
   - Subheadline: for whom, how (mechanism), without what
   - CTA with a benefit ("Reserve your spot in the cohort")
   - Micro-proof (customer count, rating, logos) + scarcity/urgency note
2. **Problem** — the customer's pain in their words; the cost of staying where they are
3. **Why previous solutions didn't work** — without attacking competitors; shift blame from the customer to the method
4. **Solution / mechanism** — named method, 3–5 steps, how it shortens time and reduces effort
5. **Proof** — outcome-focused testimonials, case studies (before → after), numbers
6. **What exactly you get (Value Stack)** — components with name, outcome, valuation
7. **Bonuses** — each with name, the obstacle it removes, and valuation (+ any limit)
8. **Guarantee** — named, full wording, visually highlighted
9. **Price / packages** — total value anchor → price or options; recommended option; guarantee next to price
10. **Scarcity and urgency** — constraint + reason why + date/number
11. **Who this is for / who it's not for** — qualification builds trust
12. **FAQ** — objections: price, time, "will it work for me", risk (link to guarantee), what happens after purchase
13. **Final CTA** — 1–2 sentence stack summary + guarantee + constraint + button
14. (optional) **P.S.** — two paths: stay with the problem, or act with a risk-free guarantee

## B. Larger website (multiple pages)

- **Home page**: hero promising the outcome for the main avatar, mechanism summary, proof, entry points to offers, CTA to the main offer.
- **Offer/product page**: full structure A (each offer = a separate Grand Slam Offer if avatars differ).
- **Pricing page**: options/packages with a value anchor, guarantee on each option, recommended option, pricing FAQ. Pricing is never the first thing on the home page.
- **About**: story in service of credibility (why we can deliver the outcome), not a CV.
- **Case studies / testimonials**: before → after, with numbers.
- **Multi-locale sites**: write in the agreed locale(s) only; keep the offer name, guarantee, and constraints consistent across locales and pages; adapt idioms, currency, and examples to each market rather than translating literally.
- Consistency: the same offer name, guarantee, and constraints across all pages.

## C. Writing rules
- Address one person directly, using the form of address agreed for the copy language.
- Benefit > feature. Translate every feature: "… so that you …".
- Specific > vague: numbers, dates, names.
- Short paragraphs, scannable headings, one idea per sentence.
- Use the customer's words from the interview and testimonials.
- CTA: verb + benefit ("Get your implementation plan", "Claim your spot"), not "Submit" / "Click here".
- Any missing data = placeholder in the copy language (e.g. `[TO FILL IN: description]`), never invented.
- Write natively in the copy language — idioms, rhythm, and punctuation of that language; never a word-for-word translation from English. "Grand Slam Offer" is the framework's name, not customer-facing text.
